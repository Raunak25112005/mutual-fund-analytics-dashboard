# Day 6 Advanced Insights

## 1. Funds with Higher Downside Risk

The VaR and CVaR analysis highlighted that a few mid-cap and small-cap schemes experienced larger negative daily returns compared to most large-cap funds. This suggests that although these funds may offer higher return potential, investors should be prepared for greater short-term volatility during unfavorable market conditions.

## 2. Strongest Risk-Adjusted Performers

When returns were adjusted for risk using the Sharpe Ratio, Mirae Asset Large Cap Fund emerged as one of the strongest performers. This indicates that the fund was able to generate comparatively better returns for each unit of risk taken by investors.

## 3. Investor Behaviour Across Cohorts

The cohort analysis showed that investors who entered the market earlier accounted for a significantly larger share of total investments. These investors also demonstrated more consistent participation, suggesting that longer market exposure often leads to greater capital accumulation over time.

## 4. SIP Continuity Patterns

A large number of investors were identified as "At-Risk" based on the average gap between SIP transactions. This could indicate missed contributions, temporary pauses in investing, or irregular investment behaviour. Maintaining consistent SIP contributions remains important for achieving long-term wealth creation through rupee-cost averaging.

## 5. Portfolio Concentration Differences

The HHI analysis revealed noticeable differences in portfolio concentration among funds. Some schemes were highly diversified across sectors and holdings, while others were more concentrated in a smaller set of stocks. Concentrated portfolios may deliver stronger returns when their chosen sectors perform well, but they can also expose investors to higher stock-specific risk.
